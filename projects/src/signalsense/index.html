What this is

Signal Sense studies how standard mobile broadband measurement, operator KPIs and crowdsourced platforms such as Ookla, structurally renders marginalized urban communities invisible. The field site is Korail, an informal settlement of about 200,000 people inside Gulshan Thana, one of Dhaka's wealthiest administrative units: the Thana average reads healthy while Korail's experience disappears into it.

Three measurement failures

  • Administrative aggregation: the spatial unit is too coarse, so Korail dissolves into Gulshan.
  • Crowdsourced undersampling: poorer residents are not sampled, so Korail is absent from the data.
  • Reference-point mismatch: standard tools measure toward their own reference servers, not the content paths residents actually depend on.

QoE as a path, not a number

  • Plane 1 · Radio & Access: can the phone get onto the network (RSRP, RSRQ, SINR, RAT, cell).
  • Plane 2 · Core & Interconnection: does the operator's own network add delay (DNS, TCP and TLS setup).
  • Plane 3 · Inter-domain & Content Delivery: where content is served from: on-net cache, in-country or international transit.
  • Human overlay: what people were trying to do and how they coped, captured through a diary study and interviews.

The Warbler Android app measures the three planes. Each Plane 2/3 burst probes near and far targets back-to-back on the same cell, so the shared floor (Planes 1+2) and the transit penalty (Plane 3) can be separated by comparison.

Components

  • Drive tests in Korail and adjacent Gulshan/Banani, on different days and times of day.
  • Participant study: about 15 Korail residents' phones running Warbler, paired with a diary study.
  • Network planner interviews with mobile operators in Bangladesh.

People: Tauhid Nabi (lead), Prof. Shaddi Hasan (PI, Virginia Tech), Jocelyn Bliton (Warbler app), Rahul Roy (field coordinator, Dhaka).
