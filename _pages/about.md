<p>I am a final-year Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Computer Science at <a href="https://www.vt.edu/">Virginia Tech</a>, advised by <a href="https://shaddih.com/">Professor Shaddi Hasan</a>. I also work closely with <a href="https://taejoong.github.io/">Professor Tijay Chung</a> and <a href="https://www.ishtiaque.net/">Professor Syed Ishtiaque Ahmed</a> (University of Toronto). I expect to complete my Ph.D. in May 2027. My research focuses on <strong>Internet Measurement, Mobile Network Infrastructure, and Internet Data Analytics</strong>, combining large-scale network measurements, statistical methods, and machine learning to study Internet performance, connectivity, and resilience.</p>

<p>My research has been published and presented at ACM IMC, ACM SIGCOMM, and IEEE Access. At <a href="https://conferences.sigcomm.org/imc/2024/">ACM IMC 2024</a>, I presented my first-authored full paper, <em>“Red is Sus,”</em> which developed a machine learning approach to identify low-quality ISP service availability claims in the <a href="https://broadbandmap.fcc.gov/">U.S. National Broadband Map</a>. We analyzed millions of location-level broadband availability claims nationwide and constructed a labeled dataset of over 750,000 observations across 911 ISPs by combining crowdsourced speed test measurements with regulatory data. Our machine learning classifier achieved an ROC AUC above 0.98, demonstrating how data-driven methods can help identify questionable broadband availability claims and support more targeted regulatory review.</p>

<p>My ongoing research includes <strong>NetBreakouts</strong>, which develops measurement-driven methods to identify and map mobile network egress infrastructure using large-scale traceroute, BGP, and IPv6 topology analysis. I also investigate <strong>IP geolocation accuracy</strong> across fixed and mobile networks, evaluating major geolocation databases using measurements from 175 countries. Another project, <strong>Signal Sense</strong>, examines mobile broadband inequality and Quality of Experience (QoE), combining crowdsourced network measurements with user-centered research. Early findings from NetBreakouts and Signal Sense have been presented at the <a href="https://conferences.sigcomm.org/imc/2025/events/prime/">ACM IMC 2025 PRIME Workshop</a> and <a href="https://conferences.sigcomm.org/sigcomm/2026/">ACM SIGCOMM 2026</a>, respectively. I was also selected as a <strong>2026 Internet Society Pulse Fellow</strong> in the Internet Resilience category.</p>

<p>During Summer 2026, I worked as a <strong>Ph.D. Data Science and Research Intern at Cisco ThousandEyes</strong>, contributing to Internet measurement research involving cellular network performance, mobile telemetry, and large-scale network event detection. Before pursuing my Ph.D., I spent approximately eight years in the telecommunications industry, working in mobile network planning, optimization, and performance analytics. This experience provides a practical foundation for my research on Internet infrastructure and network measurement.</p>

<p>I am currently seeking <strong>full-time research opportunities in industry and academia</strong>, including Research Scientist, Applied Scientist, and research-oriented Data Scientist positions. I am particularly interested in roles involving Internet measurement, network infrastructure, mobile systems, and large-scale data analytics, where I can apply my research and industry experience to develop practical measurement methods and data-driven tools for understanding Internet infrastructure, performance, and reliability.</p>
